## Executive Summary

### Platform Overview

The RESOLVE Interactive Mapping Platform represents a groundbreaking digital infrastructure for Lebanese agriculture—a free, publicly accessible, bilingual web-based tool that transforms fragmented farmer data into actionable spatial intelligence. As Lebanon's first comprehensive agricultural mapping system, the platform bridges critical information gaps that have historically hampered effective planning, resource allocation, and farmer support in the country's agricultural sector.

### Vision & Purpose

In a country where agricultural data is scattered, inaccessible, or simply non-existent, the RESOLVE platform serves as a **central nervous system** for agricultural decision-making. It answers fundamental questions that stakeholders previously could not address:

- *Where* are farmers experiencing water scarcity?
- *Which* villages have concentrations of regenerative agriculture practitioners?
- *How many* farmers are interested in specific training programs, and where are they located?
- *What* energy sources do farmers rely on, and where are alternative energy interventions most needed?
- *Who* is growing specific crops or using traditional food processing techniques?

By making this information visible, searchable, and spatially organized, the platform empowers:

- **Farmers** to discover nearby resources, cooperatives, and peer practitioners
- **NGOs and Development Organizations** to target interventions efficiently based on need and opportunity
- **Government Extension Services** to plan service delivery routes and prioritize high-need areas
- **Cooperatives** to identify potential members and understand market opportunities
- **Researchers** to design studies grounded in agricultural realities rather than assumptions
- **Policymakers** to craft evidence-based strategies supported by comprehensive baseline data
- **International Donors** to allocate funds strategically using transparent, verifiable information

## 1. Context & Need Assessment

### Agricultural Data Landscape in Lebanon

Prior to RESOLVE, Lebanon's agricultural sector operated in an information vacuum. While agricultural activity constitutes a significant portion of rural livelihoods, comprehensive data on farmer practices, resource availability, and production systems was virtually non-existent in accessible, actionable formats.

**Historical Data Gaps:**

**Government Statistical Limitations:**
- **Agricultural Census:** Last comprehensive census conducted in 2010 (16 years outdated by project end)
- **Aggregation Level:** Government statistics report national or governorate totals masking local variation
- **Access Barriers:** Reports published in Arabic only, limited digital availability, minimal spatial detail
- **Update Frequency:** Infrequent surveys (5-10 year gaps) providing outdated snapshots rather than dynamic tracking

**Research Data Fragmentation:**
- **Academic Silos:** University research data rarely shared beyond individual publications
- **Temporal Gaps:** Studies capture moments in time without longitudinal follow-up
- **Geographic Limits:** Research focused on accessible areas near universities, leaving remote regions understudied
- **Language Barriers:** Much Lebanese agricultural research published in French or English, inaccessible to Arabic-speaking practitioners

**NGO Information Hoarding:**
- **Project-Specific Data:** Development organizations collect beneficiary data but rarely share publicly
- **Overlap Ignorance:** NGOs often unaware of parallel programs in same areas leading to duplication
- **Donor Constraints:** Funding agreements sometimes restrict data sharing reducing collective intelligence
- **Format Incompatibility:** Each organization uses different data structures preventing aggregation

**Farmer Knowledge Isolation:**
- **Dispersed Expertise:** Farmers with valuable experience or traditional knowledge operate in isolation
- **No Discovery Mechanism:** Peer farmers seeking specific information lack ways to identify who possesses it
- **Weak Networks:** Extension services under-resourced, farmer organizations fragmented limiting horizontal knowledge flow

### Critical Planning Challenges Without Spatial Data

The absence of comprehensive, accessible, spatially-organized agricultural data created severe inefficiencies across the sector:

**For Development Organizations:**

*Targeting Inefficiency:*
- Organizations allocated resources based on anecdotal information, political connections, or guesswork rather than documented need
- Example: Solar panel programs conducted blanket awareness campaigns rather than targeting farmers with highest diesel costs and poorest grid access
- Result: Resources reached random beneficiaries rather than those who would benefit most

*Duplication Waste:*
- Multiple NGOs unknowingly conducted similar interventions in same villages while leaving other areas underserved
- No coordination mechanism to identify complementary programming opportunities
- Example: Three organizations separately distributed vegetable seeds in village A while village B received no agricultural support

*Baseline and M&E Challenges:*
- Projects lacked pre-intervention data making impact attribution impossible
- Evaluation relied on self-reported beneficiary data without comparable control areas
- Learning from failures difficult because contextual factors (soil type, water availability, farmer experience) not systematically documented

**For Government Extension Services:**

*Service Delivery Rationalization:*
- Extension agents visited villages on rotating schedules regardless of need intensity or crop calendar timing
- No spatial data revealing where specific crop systems, pests, or soil issues concentrated
- Agent time spread thinly across vast territories rather than prioritizing where technical support most needed

*Resource Allocation Blindness:*
- Government programs (input subsidies, irrigation infrastructure, credit) allocated through political patronage networks rather than spatial needs assessment
- No ability to identify underserved areas or populations requiring affirmative targeting

**For Farmers:**

*Resource Discovery Gap:*
- Farmers unaware of nearby cooperatives, input suppliers, markets, or extension programs they could access
- No mechanism to discover peer farmers with relevant experience (e.g., "Who nearby grows strawberries organically?")
- Traditional knowledge held by elder farmers in one village unknown to young farmers in next village

*Advocacy Limitations:*
- Farmers' voices weak in policy dialogues lacking aggregate data demonstrating collective challenges
- Example: Individual farmers complain about water scarcity but no spatial data showing systematic pattern across region elevating issue to policy priority

**For Researchers:**

*Study Design Constraints:*
- Researchers worked with convenience samples (easily accessible farmers) limiting generalizability
- No baseline data on agricultural practice distribution informing representative sampling
- Each new study expended resources gathering foundational information rather than building on existing knowledge

*Research-Practice Gap:*
- Academic findings published in journals farmers and practitioners never access
- No feedback loop showing researchers what questions remain unanswered in field reality

**For Policymakers:**

*Evidence-Based Policy Vacuum:*
- National agricultural strategies drafted without granular data on what farmers actually grow, what challenges they face, where interventions are most needed
- Policies imported from other countries without grounding in Lebanese agricultural diversity
- Example: Government promoted greenhouse intensification without data on which regions had electricity reliability to power greenhouses

### RESOLVE Platform as Solution

The RESOLVE Interactive Mapping Platform directly addresses these systemic information gaps through:

1. **Centralization:** Aggregating dispersed data into single accessible repository
2. **Standardization:** Common data structure enabling comparison and aggregation across sources
3. **Spatialization:** Geographic organization revealing patterns invisible in tabular data
4. **Democratization:** Free public access eliminating information asymmetries between actors
5. **Sustainability:** Zero-cost architecture and institutional hosting ensuring long-term availability
6. **Bilingual Accessibility:** Arabic and English removing language barriers to adoption
7. **Interactivity:** User-driven exploration rather than static reports meeting diverse stakeholder needs

## 2. Platform Architecture & Design

### Design Philosophy

The RESOLVE platform architecture reflects deliberate design choices prioritizing **accessibility, sustainability, and simplicity** over technical sophistication.

**Core Principles:**

**1. Accessibility First:**
- **No barriers to entry:** No registration, subscription, software download, or technical expertise required
- **Universal device compatibility:** Functions on any device with modern web browser—desktop, laptop, tablet, smartphone
- **Bandwidth sensitivity:** Optimized for Lebanese internet speeds (data caching reduces load times after first visit)
- **Bilingual by design:** Language not an afterthought but fundamental architectural feature

**2. Financial Sustainability:**
- **Zero recurring costs:** No server hosting fees, database licensing, or maintenance contracts
- **No vendor lock-in:** Open-source technologies prevent dependency on commercial providers who might raise prices or discontinue services
- **University absorption:** Minimal technical overhead (domain name, occasional troubleshooting) absorbable within AUB departmental budgets

**3. Technical Simplicity:**
- **Static architecture:** No complex server backend requiring specialized programming expertise to maintain
- **GitHub hosting:** Leverages free, reliable infrastructure used by millions of open-source projects worldwide
- **Standard web technologies:** HTML, CSS, JavaScript—skills widely available if technical capacity turns over
- **Comprehensive documentation:** Code comments and user guides enable future teams to understand and modify platform

**4. Data Sovereignty:**
- **Local control:** Data files stored in project repository, not third-party proprietary database
- **Version history:** Every data update tracked via Git version control enabling rollback if errors introduced
- **Export freedom:** Data downloadable in standard formats (GeoJSON, CSV) preventing platform lock-in
- **Farmer consent:** All mapped farmers verbally consented to data sharing with sensitive information excluded

**5. Scalability by Design:**
- **Modular structure:** New data layers added without rewriting existing code
- **Geographic expansion:** Same platform structure replicable in other regions with minimal adaptation
- **Performance optimization:** Clustering algorithm handles thousands of map markers smoothly even on older devices
- **Future enhancement ready:** Architecture supports adding features (search, filtering, data submission) incrementally

### Technology Stack (Non-Technical Explanation)

**Map Visualization Engine: MapLibre GL**
- **What it does:** Renders interactive maps in web browsers with smooth zoom, pan, and feature interactions
- **Why chosen:** Free, open-source (no licensing fees); modern high-performance; active development community
- **User experience:** Comparable to Google Maps—intuitive navigation familiar to most users

**Data Format: GeoJSON**
- **What it is:** Standard file format for geographic data readable by all mapping software
- **Structure:** Combines location coordinates (latitude/longitude) with descriptive information (farm characteristics)
- **Advantage:** Human-readable text files (not proprietary binary) enabling transparency and easy editing

**Caching Technology: IndexedDB**
- **What it does:** Stores data in user's web browser after first visit
- **Benefit:** Subsequent visits load instantly from local storage without re-downloading data
- **Privacy:** Data controlled by user's browser, not tracking cookies
- **Expiry:** Automatic refresh after 7 days ensuring users see updated information

**Hosting: GitHub Pages**
- **What it is:** Free web hosting service provided by GitHub (Microsoft-owned) for open-source projects
- **Reliability:** Enterprise-grade infrastructure with 99.9%+ uptime
- **Cost:** Permanently free for public projects (RESOLVE qualifies)
- **Bandwidth:** Unlimited visitors, no traffic caps or overage fees

**Code Languages:**
- **JavaScript:** Programming language making map interactive (handles clicks, filtering, language switching)
- **HTML:** Structures page layout (sidebar controls, map container, information panels)
- **CSS:** Styles visual appearance (colors, fonts, button designs, responsive layouts)
- **All standard technologies:** No exotic frameworks requiring specialized expertise

**Data Storage: Git Version Control**
- **What it does:** Tracks every change to platform code and data files with full history
- **Benefit:** Enables collaboration (multiple people updating data), mistake recovery (revert if error), transparency (audit trail)
- **User-friendly:** GitHub web interface allows non-programmers to update data via simple forms

## 3. Data Structure & Content

### Bilingual Data Architecture

The platform's most innovative structural feature is its **canonical bilingual data model**—a technical architecture enabling seamless language switching without data duplication or performance penalties.

**The Challenge:**
Traditional bilingual systems load separate data files per language (e.g., `Water_English.geojson` and `Water_Arabic.geojson`). This approach creates problems:
- **Storage Duplication:** Identical information stored twice, doubling file sizes
- **Bandwidth Waste:** Users pay double data charges downloading redundant information
- **Synchronization Risk:** Updates to one language file must mirror exactly in other—easy to desynchronize creating inconsistencies
- **Switching Delay:** Changing language requires reloading entire dataset from network

**RESOLVE Solution: Single Bilingual Files**

Each mapped feature stores values in *both* languages within a nested structure:

```json
{
  "properties": {
    "featureId": "Water_15_a3f8b9c1",
    "theme": "water",
    "values": {
      "ar": {
        "القرية": "مروستي",
        "مصدر المياه": "بئر",
        "تقييم توفر المياه": "نادر"
      },
      "en": {
        "القرية": "Mrosti",
        "مصدر المياه": "Well",
        "تقييم توفر المياه": "Scarce"
      }
    }
  }
}
```

**How It Works:**
- Platform loads bilingual data file once on first visit
- Language toggle simply switches which nested values display (`values.ar` vs. `values.en`)
- Zero network delay—language switching is instant
- Perfect synchronization guaranteed—both languages in same file, impossible to desynchronize
- 50% storage reduction compared to dual-file approach

**Benefits:**
- **User Experience:** Instant language switching encourages exploration by bilingual users
- **Data Integrity:** Single source of truth eliminates versioning conflicts
- **Future-Proof:** Structure supports adding more languages (French, Armenian) with minimal changes
- **Accessibility:** Makes platform genuinely usable for both Arabic and international audiences without compromise

### Stable Feature Identification System

Each mapped farm receives a **unique, stable identifier** enabling long-term tracking even if farmer details change.

**ID Structure:** `{Theme}_{RowNumber}_{CoordinateHash}`

Example: `Water_15_a3f8b9c1`
- **Theme:** Water (which data layer)
- **Row Number:** 15 (position in original survey dataset)
- **Coordinate Hash:** a3f8b9c1 (cryptographic hash of GPS coordinates)

**Purpose:**
- **Longitudinal Tracking:** If farmer re-surveyed in future, same ID enables before/after comparison
- **Cross-Theme Linking:** Same farmer appears in multiple themes with consistent base ID
- **Data Integrity:** Prevents duplicate entries for same farm
- **Citation:** Researchers can reference specific farm IDs in publications

**Privacy Protection:**
- IDs are anonymous—don't contain farmer name or sensitive information
- Can't reverse-engineer precise GPS coordinates from hash
- Farmer can request removal by ID if desired

### Data Collection Methodology

Understanding platform data's credibility requires transparency about collection process:

**Survey Administration:**

*Enumerator Training:*
- 6 local data collectors recruited from agricultural communities
- 2-day training on survey protocol, informed consent, data recording
- Fluent in Arabic with agricultural terminology knowledge
- Each assigned specific geographic area (home region) leveraging local trust

*Farmer Recruitment:*
- **Purposive sampling:** Targeted diversity in farm types, sizes, practices
- **Snowball technique:** Asked surveyed farmers to recommend peers with different characteristics
- **Cooperative partnerships:** Utilized cooperative membership lists (with permission)
- **Gender intentional:** Explicitly recruited women farmers (30%+ target)

*Interview Process:*
- **In-person visits:** Face-to-face 60-90 minute conversations
- **Digital tablets:** Real-time data entry into mobile survey app
- **GPS waypoints:** Coordinates captured via smartphone (farmer consent obtained)
- **Verification photos:** Farm and crop pictures (where farmer approved) validating responses
- **Informed consent:** Verbal agreement explaining data would be publicly mapped

*Quality Control:*
- **Daily data review:** Supervisor checked for incomplete or inconsistent entries
- **Follow-up calls:** 15% of farmers re-contacted to verify ambiguous responses
- **GPS validation:** Coordinates checked against satellite imagery ensuring plausibility
- **Duplicate detection:** Cross-checked against existing records preventing multiple entries for same farm

**Data Cleaning & Validation:**

*Coordinate Correction:*
- Some farmers provided village center coordinates rather than specific farm location
- **Staggering algorithm applied:** When multiple farms shared identical coordinates (same village), applied circular offset pattern (22-meter radius) preventing marker overlap while preserving village-level accuracy

*Property Standardization:*
- Survey responses had spelling variations, abbreviations, inconsistent terminology
- **Controlled vocabulary mapping:** Harmonized responses to standard categories (e.g., "تفاح", "التفاح", "تفاحة" all mapped to "تفاح" = apple)
- **Unit conversion:** Standardized measurements (liters, dunums, months) into consistent formats

*Missing Data Handling:*
- Some survey questions unanswered (farmer didn't know, refused to answer, not applicable)
- **Explicit coding:** "Unknown" or "Not specified" rather than leaving blank preventing ambiguity
- **Optional fields:** Non-critical properties allowed to be missing without excluding entire farm record

*Bilingual Translation:*
- Professional translators (native Arabic, fluent English) translated survey responses
- **Cultural adaptation:** Not literal word-for-word but conceptually equivalent terminology
- **Agricult specialist review:** Agronomist verified technical term accuracy

*Audit Trail:*
- Every data processing step documented in `data/canonical_audit/` folder
- **Audit reports:** JSON files recording source CSV row counts, column lists, transformation rules applied
- **Reproducibility:** Any stakeholder can verify data integrity by checking audit logs

**Case Study 2: Extension Service Route Optimization**

   - Timeliness (crop stages requiring immediate intervention)

## 8. Lessons Learned & Best Practices

### Technical Design Lessons

**1. Simplicity Over Sophistication**

*Lesson:*
Resisting temptation to build complex backend database with user authentication, real-time syncing, advanced analytics initially was correct decision.

*Rationale:*
- Static architecture reduced development time from 12 months to 4 months
- Eliminated ongoing server costs ($5,000+/year saved)
- Reduced maintenance burden (no security patches, database optimization, backup routines)
- Framework-less approach means no obsolescence risk from dependency upgrades

*Applicability:*
Projects with limited technical capacity and uncertain long-term funding should prioritize simple, robust solutions over feature-rich but fragile systems.

**2. Bilingual from Day One**

*Lesson:*
Architecting bilingual data structure initially (rather than launching English then adding Arabic) prevented costly refactoring.

*Rationale:*
- Retrofitting single-language systems to multilingual requires restructuring all data and interfaces
- Users frustrated by half-translated interfaces (some labels English, some Arabic)
- Translation as afterthought results in poor linguistic quality (literal translation vs. cultural adaptation)

*Applicability:*
International development projects should budget for full multilingual design upfront, not treat localization as future enhancement.

**3. Mobile-Responsive Essential**

*Lesson:*
Even platforms designed for "professional users" (NGO staff, researchers) must function on mobile devices.

*Reality Check:*
- 60% of platform traffic from smartphones/tablets
- Stakeholders traveling to villages access platform during field visits on mobile
- Donor representatives checking platform during site visits use phones
- Assumed desktop-centric use pattern wrong

*Applicability:*
Rural/agricultural technology should assume mobile-first access even when target users have office computers.

### Data Collection Lessons

**4. Local Enumerators Critical**

*Lesson:*
Hiring enumerators from survey areas rather than external neutral data collectors yielded higher quality data and farmer responsiveness.

*Advantages:*
- Enumerators knew village geography, social dynamics, agricultural context
- Farmers trusted local community members more than strangers
- Dialectal fluency (Lebanese Arabic nuances) prevented miscommunication
- Enumerators could interpret ambiguous responses drawing on contextual knowledge

*Trade-off:*
Some concern about bias (enumerators might favor friends/family) but data validation protocols caught few bias issues.

**5. Survey Length vs. Depth Trade-off**

*Challenge:*
200-question comprehensive surveys yielded rich data but limited sample size (farmer fatigue, enumerator time/cost).

*Alternative Considered:*
Shorter 50-question surveys enabling 100+ farmers mapped.

*Decision:*
Chose depth over breadth. Comprehensive profiles enable sophisticated analysis and validation (cross-checking responses for consistency).

*Reflection:*
Appropriate choice for research-oriented project. For operational program needing wider coverage, shorter survey preferable.

### Engagement & Adoption Lessons

**6. Training Investments Pay Off**

*Observation:*
Stakeholders who attended hands-on workshops use platform 3x more than those who only received email announcement.

*Implication:*
Technology introduction requires active onboarding, not "build it and they will come."

*Effective Training Elements:*
- **Practical exercises:** Participants solved real problems during training (not passive demo watching)
- **Role-specific content:** Different workshops for different user types (farmers vs. NGOs vs. researchers)
- **Takeaway materials:** Quick reference cards participants kept
- **Follow-up support:** Email helpline during first month answering user questions

**7. Champion Cultivation**

*Pattern:*
Platform adoption spreads through networks from enthusiastic early adopters who evangelize to peers.

*Strategy:*
- Identify naturally enthusiastic users (e.g., innovative NGO staff, tech-savvy extension agents)
- Provide extra support and attention building their expertise
- Invite champions to co-present at workshops sharing testimonials
- Empower champions as trainers for their own organizations/networks

*Result:*
Organic adoption more sustainable than top-down mandates.

**8. Institutional Change Requires Patience**

*Reality:*
Individual staff members embrace platform quickly; organizational integration takes years.

*Stages Observed:*
1. **Shadow Adoption** (Month 1-6): Innovative staff use platform informally without organizational sanction
2. **Pilot Projects** (Month 6-12): Small teams use platform for specific initiatives demonstrating value
3. **Broader Awareness** (Year 1-2): Success stories circulate; more staff try platform
4. **Partial Integration** (Year 2-3): Platform referenced in some planning documents; inconsistent use
5. **Full Institutionalization** (Year 3-5): Platform formally integrated into SOPs, budgets, training

*Implication:*
Projects should plan 3-5 year adoption timelines for institutional change, not expect immediate transformation.

### Sustainability Lessons

**9. Zero-Cost Imperative**

*Lesson:*
Platforms dependent on ongoing funding rarely survive past initial project.

*Lebanese Context:*
- Economic crisis makes even small recurring fees unaffordable
- Donor priorities shift unpredictably
- Government budget constraints preclude public sector hosting costs

*Solution:*
Architecting platform with zero marginal cost per user and minimal fixed costs (~$500/year) ensures sustainability without continuous fundraising.

*Applicability:*
Resource-constrained contexts should prioritize cost-minimization over feature-maximization.

**10. Multi-Institutional Hosting Resilience**

*Lesson:*
Single-institution dependency creates risk (organization deprioritizes, loses capacity, faces political pressure).

*Strategy:*
- Primary host (AUB) with governance oversight
- International mirror (MetaMeta) providing redundancy
- Open data and code enabling emergency migration if needed

*Result:*
Platform survives even if one host fails.

### Data Governance Lessons

**11. Farmer Consent Foundation**

*Approach:*
Every surveyed farmer verbally consented to data public sharing with explanation of platform purpose.

*Benefit:*
- Ethical foundation defensible against privacy critiques
- Farmers feel ownership and pride seeing their data used constructively
- No legal liability concerns about unauthorized data use

*Ongoing Requirement:*
Consent process documented (enumerator signature + farmer name confirming consent obtained). Removal protocol if farmer later objects (~2 requests in 18 months).

**12. Transparency Builds Trust**

*Practice:*
All platform decisions (data additions, policy changes, feature priorities) discussed openly with advisory committee and meeting minutes published.

*Alternative:*
Closed decision-making by hosting institution would be faster but erode stakeholder buy-in.

*Benefit:*
Transparent governance creates collective ownership. Stakeholders defend platform because they shape it.
